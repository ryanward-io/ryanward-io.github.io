From 23 to 27 September we drove our BYD Atto 3 (50 kWh battery) from Melbourne to Bright, on to Canberra and then back to Melbourne, with the kids in the back. It has a CCS2 connector, which was the predominant charger type we saw on our trip and as I understand it's effectively the universal standard now.

Charging while on the road took eight stops and cost $177.32 in total. Here is what it looked like in numbers, and then how the days went.

Costs are from my bank transactions; energy is shown in the apps with two here approximated.

Prices ranged from $0.75 per kWh at Glenrowan North to $0.85 at Tarcutta, apart from the Tesla charger at Coolac, which worked out at roughly $0.55. This is high but expected - at home it's closer to $0.20 per kWh. While chargers were often rated 150 kW to ~300 kW, the Atto 3 can take at most 70 kW and the average actual charge was about 50 kW, due to other cars using the infrastructure or the power slowing to protect the battery when I charged over 80%.

## Day by day

### Wednesday 23 September: Melbourne to Bright

The first charge was at the Ampol Foodary in Avenel on a 150 kW Evie charger, from 1:28 to 1:56 pm. It delivered 24.57 kWh and cost $19.91.

At 3:49 pm, 1 hour 53 minutes after that session ended, we plugged in at bp pulse Glenrowan North on the Hume Freeway. In 24 minutes it delivered 21.52 kWh for $16.14, leaving the battery at 85%, plenty to get to Bright.

For both of these charge spots, we let the kids out, stretched legs, went to the toilet, ate food and played with equipment. For neither of these were we here waiting to charge longer than we wanted to be.

We arrived at Bright with about 40% or 20 kWh of charge. In retrospect we could've probably got away with a single stop but this was our first long trip so we were conservative with charging.

### Thursday 24 September: Bright

In Bright we used a 50 kW Evie charger, from 11:25 am to 12:00 pm. It delivered 25.14 kWh in 35 minutes for $19.36. It was the slowest of the six recorded sessions, averaging 43 kW.

I went solo to charge it and grabbed some groceries from IGA and lunch so again wasn't actively waiting. Otherwise we walked everywhere in Bright and didn't use the car.

### Friday 25 September: Bright to Canberra

On Friday we made two charges just tapping with card, so I don't have good data for them. First an NRMA station in Holbrook at about 10 am, which cost $29.60, and second the Tesla supercharging station outside Coolac at about 12 pm, which cost $14.59. Figuring out how to disconnect from these required some quick googling but was solved by quickly double unlocking the car.

We arrived in Canberra with about 30% and slowly charged back up to 100% using home charging (about 1 to 1.5 kW of power) over the next few days which was free to us (it would've cost our host about $7-$10).

### Saturday 26 September: Canberra

Questacon was fun!

### Sunday 27 September: Canberra to Melbourne

Three charges on the way back.

- **Tarcutta** (Ampol Foodary, 315 kW Evie): 9:38 to 10:10 am, 28.83 kWh in 31 minutes 47 seconds, $24.51.
- **Wodonga West** (7-Eleven, 150 kW Evie): 11:34 am to 12:07 pm, 30.75 kWh in 33 minutes 35 seconds, $24.29.
- **Avenel** (Ampol Foodary, 150 kW Evie): 2:23 to 3:05 pm, 35.71 kWh in 41 minutes 52 seconds, $28.92.

Avenel was the only annoying stop of the trip. There was a queue for the four charging stations and so I had to wait about 15 minutes before I started charging. We had also arrived on about 10% battery so had no real choice to wait. This was the only stop where we felt genuinely slowed down by having to charge, but we got out the colouring books and the kids had a good time.
